# MorphStack SLR: Comparison Matrix

Searches run on 2026-11-04 (see `protocol.md`). Marks: ✓ = yes, ✗ = no, partial = see Notes.

**What each column means (applied to activation state, not to ordinary data):**
- **Stack / Segmented / Heap**: the work uses or evaluates that representation of activation records (contiguous or growable stack; linked segments or stacklets; heap frames, heap continuations, or heap state machines).
- **Migration**: live activation state moves from one representation to another at run time.
- **Bidirectional**: migration happens in both directions (for example, stack to heap *and* heap to stack).
- **Per Activation**: the decision or migration happens per frame. The alternatives are whole thread, whole stack, or whole program.
- **Online Policy**: a decision is driven by *observed cost or behaviour*. A mechanism forced by language semantics (capture, suspend, overflow) or a fixed compile-time choice does not count.
- **Oracle Comparison**: the work evaluates against a hindsight or offline optimum (competitive ratio, regret, or offline-OPT baseline).

For theory papers (group F), the three representation columns are ✗ by definition. Their Online and Oracle columns describe the framework.

Unless a row says "full text read", it was screened from the abstract, metadata, and secondary sources only.

## Summary of coverage

- **No row has ✓ in all of Migration, Bidirectional, Per Activation, Online Policy, and Oracle Comparison** while also covering the activation-state representations.
- **Per-activation bidirectional stack↔heap migration** already exists (Hieb et al. 1990, Cog VM, Stadler et al. 2009, Loom freeze/thaw with lazy return-barrier copying). In every case it is *forced by semantics* (capture, reification, park/unpark, page exhaustion) and is not a cost-driven choice.
- **Three-way representation comparison** exists (Clinger et al. 1999; Farvardin & Reppy 2020), but only for fixed, whole-program choices. The one hybrid there is a one-way size threshold.
- **Online switching with oracle or competitive analysis** exists in theory (group F) and in other systems domains (Lin et al. 2011; OReO 2024). In runtime systems, switching exists without an oracle (Soman et al. 2004; CoCo 2013). None of it applies to activation state.
